## PRESENTING INFORMATION/REASON FOR REFERRAL

### Reason for Referral

CLIENT is a 20-year-old, right-handed female who is seeking an outpatient neuropsychological assessment in the context of notable and long-standing difficulties with time-management, sensory sensitivities (i.e., light, sound, and texture), rigid adherence to routines (e.g., getting dressed in a particular sequence), and reading comprehension beginning in early childhood. She also noted more recent difficulties with attention and concentration since starting college, which have led to heightened frustration. The purpose of this evaluation is to determine strengths and weaknesses and to assist with diagnostic clarification, treatment planning, and care.

### Presenting Complaints/Symptoms

CLIENT endorsed difficulties with a variety of cognitive, sensory, mood, and academic challenges. She described long-standing challenges with time-management beginning in early childhood. She explained that she was frequently late for the school bus throughout elementary, middle, and high school and often arrived late to her dance classes. Now that she’s in college, she noted that getting to class on time has become even more challenging, particularly because her daily schedule has less built-in structure than it did when she was in grade school. She added that it also usually takes her longer to complete tasks compared to her peers.

Regarding sensory sensitivities, CLIENT described long-standing sensitivities to sound, texture, and light beginning in early childhood. She described that she has always needed very specific conditions of quiet, light, and space to be able to focus and complete her schoolwork. While she described that she was generally able to create these ideal conditions for herself throughout childhood and adolescence, it has become increasingly difficult to manage them now that she is in college.

Regarding sensitivity to sound, CLIENT described being easily distracted by even the slightest noises, such as the air conditioning next door or people talking quietly next door. She described that during middle and high school, she struggled to do her schoolwork during free time in class, because the area was not completely quiet. She stated that hearing sounds while attempting to complete schoolwork makes it very difficult for her to concentrate. She also noted that when she is tired, frustrated, or working on a task that requires additional effort, any sound makes it particularly challenging to stay focused.

Regarding sensitivity to textures, she described that as a child she was particularly sensitive to certain textures on clothing, such as jeans, and would avoid wearing them. She described that this particular sensitivity to texture did not negatively interfere with her daily functioning, as jeans were not part of her required school uniform. She described that this sensitivity has decreased in recent years.

CLIENT described that her sensitivity to light became more noticeable since she entered college. Growing up, CLIENT’s mother described that CLIENT always gravitated towards sunshine when she was a child. CLIENT described that her home growing up had high ceilings and open space, which allowed for ample natural lighting. As a result, finding well-lit settings to do her homework was not a major challenge for her during childhood. This changed when she moved away for college. Specifically, she explained that she currently cannot focus in certain parts of her school building that lack bright natural light, and that poor lighting negatively impacts both her attention and her mood.

CLIENT endorsed longstanding challenges with rigid adherence to routines, which she described she did not recognize these patterns in herself until last year. Specifically, she described that last year she felt compelled to shower every day and would feel “uncomfortable” if she did not. She described a strong need for things to feel “just right,” such as decorating the mantel in a specific way, arranging chairs in a specific configuration during meetings, or getting dressed in a precise sequence. Her mother confirmed that these patterns have been present since childhood, noting that CLIENT has always preferred doing things in a particular order, including getting dressed, ever since grade school.

CLIENT reported increased difficulties with attention and concentration, along with heightened stress and frustration since starting college. Specifically, she described that challenges with time management and sensory sensitivities often leave her with insufficient time to prepare for classes, difficulty completing assignments on time, and trouble sustaining focus. These difficulties contribute to increased frustration, stress, academic challenges, and difficulty effectively managing her symptoms.

CLIENT also endorsed lifelong challenges with reading comprehension, such that she described frequently needing to re-read sentences multiple times to fully understand them.

## RELEVANT HISTORY

All relevant history and background information were obtained through a neuropsychological history questionnaire completed by the patient, clinical interview with the patient, communications with collateral informants, and a review of available medical and academic records.

### Medical and Developmental History

Medical and developmental history are unremarkable. No significant illness, injury, or hospitalizations were endorsed. Developmental milestones were reportedly met on time. Vision and hearing are reportedly within normal limits.

### Family History

CLIENT was born in Los Angeles, California and is an only child. Her immediate family history is remarkable for high blood pressure.

### Emotional/Behavioral History

CLIENT endorsed lifelong challenges consistent of racing thoughts (i.e., frequent overthinking) and rapid mood changes. Regarding mood changes, she described that as early as childhood, she would become highly dysregulated when things did not go her way. Specifically, she would become upset, cry, and get very angry. Her mother corroborated these challenges, noting that CLIENT can shift quickly from being very upbeat and engaged to becoming completely withdrawn and requiring time to recover. CLIENT also reported additional emotional and behavioral challenges, characterized by fatigue, low motivation, anxiety, decreased need for sleep, low frustration tolerance, overwhelming need to perform certain behaviors, and rapid mood changes. CLIENT described that since last year (approximately 2023), these challenges have significantly negatively impacted her daily functioning.

CLIENT has a brief history of attending psychotherapy in 2023, completing approximately five sessions over the course of a couple of months. She discontinued her sessions due to feeling that the sessions were not sufficiently action-oriented.

Regarding behavioral functioning, as described in Presenting Symptoms/Complaints, CLIENT endorsed longstanding challenges with rigid adherence to routines, which she described she did not recognize these patterns in herself until last year. See Presenting Symptoms/Complaints for more.

Currently, CLIENT describes that she is a generally happy and content person. She described that she does not generally get super excited or super upset. She is not currently taking any psychopharmacological medications. She denied a history of substance abuse or alcohol/tobacco abuse and dependence. She denied past or current suicidal or homicidal ideation.

### Social History

Throughout her early development, CLIENT was noted to demonstrate appropriate play and social interests. Parents endorsed intact ability making friends and maintaining appropriate social boundaries. Parents also endorsed an intact understanding of others’ emotions. Parents reported that CLIENT frequently had playdates during early childhood. However, once she entered grade school, she had fewer playdates due to increased involvement in multiple extracurricular activities (e.g., dance and Girl Scouts).

CLIENT described that throughout middle and high school, she had a large group of friends but did not have any especially close individual friendships. She described that it was challenging finding the time to spend time with them during school.

CLIENT shared that she had a close group of friends during her freshman year of college, which she greatly enjoyed; however, this group dissolved over time. Currently, CLIENT remains socially active in college but has not yet developed close, one-on-one friendships. She shared that she enjoys socializing with peers by spending time together, sharing meals, attending clubs and parties, and participating in group social activities. She noted that she especially enjoys the first week of the school year, as it offers many opportunities to meet new people. CLIENT expressed a desire to form a close friend group similar to the one she had during her freshman year. She shared that she would like friends who have similar academic and social interests as her.

Currently, CLIENT resides in her college dormitory at the University of Southern California (USC), where she is a junior. During academic breaks, she lives with her parents. She described that her daily routine entails waking up, getting dressed, going to class, coming home, eating food, doing homework, occasionally engaging in recreational activities (e.g., clubs or sorority events), and then going to bed.

### Educational and Occupational History

Per self-report and academic records, CLIENT earned mostly As throughout her schooling. Per mother report, CLIENT experienced reading challenges in 1st grade and sought tutoring services from a reading specialist from 1st through 3rd grades. Following this intervention, her reading skills were reportedly intact, although CLIENT still reports the need to reread content multiple times to fully comprehend the material.

Throughout her schooling, CLIENT described lifelong challenges with time-management, task completion, attention, and reading comprehension, which she described negatively impacted her academic work. Beginning in early childhood, she frequently arrived late to school and extracurricular activities and noted that academic tasks often took her longer to complete compared to her peers. Throughout middle and high school, she struggled to complete schoolwork during unstructured class time and reported difficulty sustaining focus, particularly in distracting environments. She added that she experienced difficulties completing exams within the allotted time during middle and high school, particularly on tests that included written responses. She described frequently feeling rushed during exams during these years.

Currently, CLIENT is a junior at USC. Since starting college, she described that these challenges have intensified due to reduced classroom structure, resulting in difficulty arriving to class on time, insufficient time to prepare for classes, problems completing assignments by deadlines, and increased difficulty maintaining attention. She described that she often needs to reread material multiple times to fully understand it. CLIENT denied any history of academic accommodations or tutoring services, with the exception of undergoing the reading intervention during early grade school.

### Previous Evaluations

CLIENT denied participating in any previous psychological or neuropsychological evaluations.
