# Contradictions and Confidence Memo

## Contradictions

| Topic | Source A | Source B | Reconciliation Decision | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Attention onset/visibility | Questionnaire implies attention/absorption problems have "kind of always been the case." | Intake notes repeatedly say focus concerns became most noticeable in college. | Treat as longstanding vulnerability whose functional visibility increased in college due to lower structure and greater self-management demands. | high |
| Current mood vs. prior distress | Questionnaire says mood in the prior two weeks was good. | Intake notes describe marked frustration, dysregulation, and impairment last year. | Treat as different time windows rather than contradiction; final draft should note current relative stability while acknowledging last year's higher burden. | high |
| Prior diagnoses | Questionnaire denies prior diagnosis by a professional. | Referral includes formal diagnostic labels. | Treat referral labels as referral coding/provisional framing, not confirmed historical diagnosis. | high |
| Education form wording | Questionnaire lists bachelor's degree while also saying USC is in progress. | Intake notes and transcript clearly show undergraduate enrollment. | Treat questionnaire degree item as form-selection noise; describe CLIENT as a current undergraduate student. | high |

## Thinly Supported Areas

- Extended family psychiatric/medical history.
- Occupational history beyond current student role.
- Detailed medical history beyond generally unremarkable self-report.
- Precise semester-by-semester transcript detail in OCR academic records.

## Evidence Limits To Surface In Final Draft

- Avoid presenting referral diagnoses as established diagnoses.
- Keep family history concise and qualified because the record is limited.
- Do not overinterpret academic OCR records beyond broad chronology, achievement level, and absence of documented supports.
