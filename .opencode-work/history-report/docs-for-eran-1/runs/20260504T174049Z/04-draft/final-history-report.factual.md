## PRESENTING INFORMATION/REASON FOR REFERRAL

### Reason for Referral

CLIENT is a 20-year-old college student who was referred for outpatient psychological/neuropsychological testing because of longstanding concerns involving attention, time management, sensory sensitivity, and rigid patterns of behavior that became more impairing in college. She described wanting clarification about how her brain functions and how that information could guide future decisions and treatment planning.

### Presenting Complaints/Symptoms

CLIENT described longstanding executive-functioning difficulty, especially chronic lateness, poor time management, and needing more time than peers to complete tasks. Examples include being late to the bus or dance classes when younger, feeling rushed on timed work, and struggling to complete some assignments on time in college.

She also described attention and concentration problems that appear longstanding but became more noticeable in college. She reported difficulty absorbing lecture material, distractibility in noisy environments, reliance on self-study, difficulty multitasking, and trouble switching between tasks.

Sensory sensitivity is part of the complaint pattern. CLIENT described longstanding sensitivity to noise, light, and texture. She reported that quiet, light, and space affect her ability to work effectively, and noise from features such as air conditioning can make concentration difficult.

CLIENT also described rigid routines and sequencing behaviors that appear longstanding and were more disruptive last year. Approved examples include showering and dressing in a specific order, rearranging items until they feel right, and becoming confused, frustrated, or unsettled when the sequence is disrupted.

These symptoms are linked to frustration, slower work completion, and greater day-to-day burden under college demands rather than to poor academic potential.

## RELEVANT HISTORY

### Medical and Developmental History

Developmental history is largely unremarkable. Milestones were reportedly met on time. Available medical history is limited but generally unremarkable. CLIENT denied major medical conditions, seizures, and concussion history, and she reported no current medications.

Sleep history suggests a delayed schedule rather than major insomnia. CLIENT reported staying up late, often because she continues doing things at night, but she also reported that she falls asleep easily and usually stays asleep.

### Family History

CLIENT is an only child. She reported living at college during the academic year and with her family during breaks. Immediate family medical history is remarkable for high blood pressure, and extended family medical history is otherwise unremarkable in the available record. Collateral history also suggests that mother has historically provided substantial structure, reminders, and accountability support.

### Emotional/Behavioral History

Emotional and behavioral history is notable for anxiety, frustration, racing thoughts or overthinking, periods of low motivation, and poor frustration tolerance. Both self-report and collateral suggest that last year was the period when these concerns were most impairing in daily life.

Approved examples include alternating periods of overextension and underextension, increased frustration around sensory demands and incomplete work, and episodes in which becoming upset led to substantial delay before she could resume functioning. Current mood was described as generally good in the recent period, and the available records do not support suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, psychiatric hospitalization, or substance misuse.

### Social History

Social motivation appears intact. CLIENT described enjoying people, clubs, parties, shared meals, and meeting new people. At the same time, the record suggests that close one-on-one friendships have been less established than broader group participation. Across development, academic and extracurricular demands appear to have limited time for social life.

### Educational and Occupational History

Educational history is notable for consistently strong achievement from secondary school through college. Middle- and high-school records support solid-to-excellent performance, with high-school GPA listed as 3.97 unweighted and 4.60 weighted. Current USC records list a 3.92 cumulative GPA in a Business Administration program.

Strong performance has coexisted with longstanding effort cost and inefficiency. CLIENT reportedly needed reading support in early elementary school, described rereading and reading-comprehension inefficiency, and reported chronic difficulty finishing timed work or completing tasks on time. She denied any history of special education services or formal accommodations. Occupational history outside her current role as a student is not meaningfully documented.

### Previous Evaluations

CLIENT denied any prior psychological or neuropsychological evaluation. She previously completed a limited number of intake-oriented therapy sessions through USC, after which testing was recommended. Referral materials documented provisional concern related to attention, obsessive-compulsive/related, and neurodevelopmental symptoms; these labels are treated here as referral framing rather than established diagnosis.
