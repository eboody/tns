## PRESENTING INFORMATION/REASON FOR REFERRAL

### Reason for Referral

CLIENT is a 20-year-old college student who is seeking an outpatient neuropsychological evaluation in the context of longstanding concerns related to attention, time management, sensory sensitivity, and rigid patterns of behavior that have become more burdensome in college. The purpose of this evaluation is to clarify cognitive and emotional strengths and weaknesses and to assist with diagnostic clarification, treatment planning, and care.

### Presenting Complaints/Symptoms

CLIENT described longstanding difficulties with attention and executive functioning, particularly time management, chronic lateness, and inefficiency in completing tasks. Specifically, she reported that she has long needed more time than peers to finish activities and has struggled to be on time since childhood, with examples including difficulty making the bus, arriving late to dance classes, and more recently feeling rushed by college demands and occasionally failing to complete work on time. Although she described these patterns as longstanding, they appear to have become more visible in college, where she has had to rely more heavily on self-directed structure and independent studying.

In addition to these executive concerns, CLIENT endorsed notable attention and concentration difficulties. She described being highly distractible, having trouble absorbing lecture material even when she is trying to pay attention, and needing to actively study or engage with material in order to learn it well. She also reported difficulty multitasking, shifting between tasks, and maintaining focus when there is competing noise in the environment. These difficulties appear to interfere primarily with efficiency rather than with intellectual potential.

CLIENT also described longstanding sensory sensitivities that appear closely tied to her concentration and day-to-day functioning. She reported sensitivity to noise, light, and clothing texture beginning in childhood, with some reduction in texture sensitivity over time. Currently, she described needing very specific conditions of quiet, lighting, and space in order to work effectively, and noted that environmental noises such as air conditioning can become intensely distracting and frustrating. Records further suggest that these sensory factors worsen her ability to focus and contribute to slowed work completion when her environment does not feel manageable.

Finally, CLIENT described rigid patterns of behavior and adherence to routines that appear to have been present for many years but became more prominent last year. Examples include showering, dressing, and arranging items in a particular order, as well as feeling unsettled or frustrated when this order is disrupted. Mother similarly reported that these preferences have been noticeable since grade school, although both she and CLIENT described them as becoming more salient and impairing during a period of heightened stress. Overall, the history suggests a longstanding pattern of attentional inefficiency, sensory sensitivity, and rigidity that has carried a greater functional burden as environmental demands have increased.

## RELEVANT HISTORY

### Medical and Developmental History

Developmental history is largely unremarkable, with milestones reportedly met on time. Available medical history is otherwise limited and generally unremarkable, as CLIENT denied major medical conditions, seizure history, and concussion history. She is not taking psychiatric medication based on the available history.

Sleep history is notable for a delayed schedule rather than a clear sleep-maintenance problem. CLIENT reported a tendency to stay up late because she continues doing things at night, with bedtimes at times extending into the early morning hours. At the same time, she described falling asleep easily and generally staying asleep, suggesting that the main issue is timing and schedule consistency rather than inability to sleep.

### Family History

CLIENT is an only child and reported living at college during the school year and with her family during breaks. Immediate family medical history is remarkable for high blood pressure, and extended family medical history is otherwise unremarkable in the available record. Collateral history further suggests that mother has historically provided substantial structure, reminders, and accountability support, which may have helped compensate for some of CLIENT's longstanding inefficiency.

### Emotional/Behavioral History

Psychiatric history is notable for anxiety, frustration, overthinking or racing thoughts, periods of low motivation, and poor frustration tolerance. CLIENT's symptom survey also endorsed emotionality, rapid mood changes, and ritualized behaviors. Across sources, last year was described as the period during which these concerns became most impairing, with increased stress related to academic demands, sensory strain, and difficulty managing responsibilities.

Records include examples of emotional dysregulation when CLIENT feels tired, rushed, or overwhelmed, including episodes in which she became so upset that it took substantial time to recover and resume functioning. At the same time, she described her recent mood as generally good, suggesting some improvement relative to the prior more difficult period. She denied suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, and prior psychiatric hospitalization, and the available records do not suggest problematic substance use beyond occasional social alcohol use.

### Social History

Social functioning appears mixed but generally intact. CLIENT described herself as someone who enjoys being around people, meeting new peers, attending clubs and parties, and sharing meals with others. Throughout development, however, she appears to have had limited time for frequent one-on-one socializing because of the combined demands of school, homework, dance, commuting, and other extracurricular activities.

Currently, CLIENT described having social groups but not the kind of close individual friendship she would like. Records suggest that she had a stronger friend group during her freshman year of college, though this later dissipated. Taken together, the history suggests preserved social interest and group participation, with comparatively less success developing close, sustained one-on-one relationships.

### Educational and Occupational History

Educational history is notable for consistently strong academic performance despite longstanding inefficiency and effort cost. CLIENT reportedly needed reading support during early elementary school because of frustration with reading and math word problems, though no formal learning disability or special education history was documented. Middle- and high-school records support solid-to-excellent academic performance, and her high-school transcript lists a 3.97 unweighted GPA and a 4.60 weighted GPA at graduation.

This strong performance has continued in college. Current USC records identify CLIENT as a Business Administration student with a Real Estate Development minor and a cumulative GPA of 3.92. Importantly, the history does not suggest that high grades reflect absence of struggle. Rather, CLIENT described a longstanding pattern of rereading, difficulty completing timed work, and needing substantial effort and compensation to keep up, all without formal accommodations. Occupational history outside of her current student role is limited in the available records.

### Previous Evaluations

CLIENT denied any prior psychological or neuropsychological evaluation. She did report several intake-oriented therapy sessions through USC, after which testing was recommended. Referral materials documented provisional concern related to attention, obsessive-compulsive/related, and neurodevelopmental symptoms; however, these labels are best understood here as referral framing rather than as established diagnoses.
